### How they fail today

Every program is rejected with Error 113 at the `case` line (statement start not recognised); the
`ERR` cases get 113 instead of their numbers. No case passes accidentally.

### Oracle findings

- Labels are constants limited to -32768..32767 (112), even for Word and LongInt selectors.
- The selector is converted to 16 bits as Integer: a Word or LongInt selector matches labels
  against its low 16 bits sign-extended.
- With overlapping or duplicate labels the first matching arm runs; `5..1` is accepted and empty.
- At least one label is required (`case i of end` and `case i of else ...` are 42); the `else` part
  is a statement list; `;` is allowed before `else` and before `end`; a second `else` is 85.
- A label whose type differs from the selector's is 74 (reported on the label's line); a variable
  label is 133.

### Intended behaviour

Parser: `case expr of arm {; arm} [; else statements] [;] end`, labels `expr[..expr]` separated by
commas. Checker: selector ordinal (39 otherwise), labels constant (133), same type family (74),
Integer range (112). Codegen: selector evaluated once into a local, narrowed to Integer; arms as
a chain of `(sel - lo) <=u (hi - lo)` tests (skipping empty ranges) inside one block, each arm
ending in `br` to the block end; the else statements follow the chain. One strategy (compare
chains) is used: correct for sparse wide ranges without tables, and shortest. At GREEN: remove the
passing programs from PENDING.
